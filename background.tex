\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{cha:literature-review}

High-dimensional multivariate count data with group structure arises across many modern applications. In computational linguistics, documents partition into authors or topics, and each document is a vocabulary-sized word-count vector \citep{Taddy_2015}. In micro-biome ecology, samples nest within subjects while the response be a vector of taxonomic abundances over hundreds of taxa \citep{Tu2023}. Despite their diversity, these settings share a common structure with $I$ observations in $J$ groups, whose individual observation is a count vector $\bm{y}_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}^Q_{\geq 0}$ over $Q$ categories.
 
The modeling challenge has two dimensions. The compositional aspect calls for the Multinomial distribution with a log-linear softmax link. The hierarchical aspect requires group-level random effects to capture dependence among observations from the same group. Ignoring this structure inflates Type~I error and introduces confounding bias in fixed-effect estimates \citep{Lee2019AnalysisOG}. The literature has addressed these two dimensions largely in isolation. The present work provides a synthesis suited to the large-$Q$ regime.
 
The simplest approach treats observations as exchangeable and attributes all variation to measured covariates via Multinomial logistic regression. For large $Q$, direct Multinomial maximum likelihood is computationally prohibitive owing to the coupled softmax normalizer. \citet{10.2307/2348134} established the Poisson--Multinomial Equivalence (PME). Conditional on the total count $M_i$, the Multinomial likelihood factors can be separated into $Q$ independent Poisson log-linear models with a common additive offset $\xi_{ij}$. \citet{Taddy_2015} exploited this to propose Distributed Multinomial Regression (DMR) and applied it to large-vocabulary document classification. \citet{lee2017poissontrickextensionsfitting} subsequently formalized and extended the PME framework clarifying conditions under which it applies to a broad class of Multinomial regression models.
 
DMR and standard MLR remain important baselines, but their central limitation is the absence of group-level random effects. When groups differ systematically, treating observations as exchangeable absorbs unmodeled group variation into fixed-effect estimates. For the large-$Q$ setting with high-dimensional fixed effects, \citet{Correia_2020} provides efficient Poisson estimation algorithms. Our work builds on this computational tradition while additionally modeling group-level structure.
 
\citet{Aitchison_1989} introduced the Poisson Lognormal (PLN) distribution placing a Gaussian prior $\bm{Z}_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}, \bm{\Sigma})$ on the log-linear predictors and drawing $Y_{ijq} \mid Z_{ijq} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\bigl(\exp(Z_{iq})\bigr)$ independently. PLN captures between-category correlations and over-dispersion. However, they can not  estimate a full $Q\times Q$ covariance $\bm{\Sigma}$ in large $Q$ regime.
 
\citet{chiquet2018variational} addressed this via PLN-PCA, which constraining $\bm{\Sigma} = \mathbf{CC}^\top$ with $\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{Q\times K}$ and $K\ll Q$, then used variational EM with a mean-field Gaussian approximation. PLN-PCA imposes a low-rank factor covariance, who has the closest structural antecedent to our model. The key distinctions are that PLN-PCA operates at the observation level only and no group random effect. Also, they provided no large-$Q$ asymptotic theory.
 
\citet{Tu2023} introduced logistic-normal multinomial (LNM) factor analyzers with a factor-structured observation-level covariance $\bm{\Sigma} = \mathbf{BB}^{\top} + \bm{\Psi}$, $\bm{\Psi} = \mathrm{diag}(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_Q)$. The EM algorithm uses a variational Gaussian approximation in the E-step and the Rubin-Thayer iterations \citep{Rubin1982} in the M-step. Their LNM-FA shares the factor-analytic motivation of the present work, which point motivated our work targeting to structure a group-level random effect $\bm{\lambda}_j = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{f}_j + \bm{\epsilon}_j$, adopting the spherical simplification $\mathbf{D} = \sigma^2\mathbf{I}_Q$. 

Relative to PLN-PCA, we add a group-level hierarchical structure, a spherical idiosyncratic term, and Laplace inference. Relative to LNM-FA, the principal advances are the group-level factor structure and the large-$Q$ consistency theory. Relative to DMR, we add group-level random effects with a factor prior, thus DMR can be recovered as the special case when $J = 1$.
