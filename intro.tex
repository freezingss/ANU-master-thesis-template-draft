\chapter{Introduction}
\label{cha:intro}

In many practical statistical applications, data are often incomplete due to missing observations, measurement corruption, or the presence of latent variables. These issues make the computation of maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) considerably more difficult than in fully observed settings. The Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm was developed to address this challenge, and its effectiveness has been further enhanced by advances in modern computing. Consequently, EM has become a fundamental method for likelihood-based inference with incomplete data, leading to an extensive body of research on its computational and statistical properties. 

However, their use in high-dimensional applications  may be very limited as both the storage requirements and the additional computational cost per-iteration can quickly become burdensome as the number of model parameters grows.
